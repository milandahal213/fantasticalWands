Guide

Build the box

A tap-to-connect controller for LEGO Education motors and sensors, built on a Raspberry Pi Pico W. Follow the steps to print, wire, and assemble it. Budget about an evening plus print time.

Parts

  • Raspberry Pi Pico W
  • WS1850S RFID/NFC reader (M5Stack RFID2)
  • 0.66" SSD1306 OLED, 64×64 (optional)
  • Passive buzzer
  • WS2812 NeoPixel (1)
  • Momentary push button
  • NTAG21x cards / the LEGO connection cards
  • Hookup wire, headers

Tools

  • 3D printer + filament
  • Soldering iron
  • USB cable (data, not charge-only)
  • Chrome or Edge on a computer

  1. Print the enclosure

    STL preview / print photo

    Download the STL files and print at 0.2 mm layer height. No supports needed for the lid; the base needs supports under the card slot.

    ⬇ base.stl ⬇ lid.stl

  2. Wire the Pico

    wiring photo

    Solder the modules to the Pico following this map:

    ModulePico pins
    NFC reader (I²C)SDA → GP26, SCL → GP27, 3V3, GND
    OLED (I²C, optional)SDA → GP4, SCL → GP5, 3V3, GND
    BuzzerGP18 → +, GND
    NeoPixelDIN → GP28, 3V3, GND
    ButtonGP20 → GND

  3. Assemble

    assembly photo

    Seat the Pico and reader in the base, route the button and NeoPixel to the lid, and snap it together. Leave the USB port accessible.

  4. Next: load the code

    With the box built and plugged in, head to the next page to flash the firmware straight from this browser.

Setup

Load the code onto your Pico

Connect the Pico (top-right), then flash. This copies every module to the board over USB and reboots it — no Thonny needed.

Not connected yet? Click Connect Pico at the top and pick the serial port. Desktop Chrome/Edge only.

Project 1

Tap & Connect

What you need: the box, a LEGO device (Single/Double Motor, Color Sensor, or Controller) powered on, and its connection card.

What to expect: tap a card and the box finds that device over Bluetooth and connects. It doesn't drive anything yet — this proves the reader and radio work. Press the box button to disconnect.

Project 2

The Bridge

The Pico bridges a source (controller or sensor) to the motors. Pick a behavior, then tap the card on each device it needs and tap the Pico — it connects to all of them and runs.

  1. Tap the connection card on each device: .
  2. Tap the card on the Pico. It scans, connects to all of them, then runs.
  3. Press the box button to stop.

Connect the Pico (top-right) to enable Start.
