Line-following robot

A robot with a color sensor learns to stay centered on a curved red-blue-green line, entirely through trial and error.

The setup

The sensor reads red (drifted one way), blue (centered), or green (drifted the other way) — that reading alone is the state, 3 states in total. Every step it picks one of three actions: turn left, go straight, or turn right. Rewards: blue is best, red/green are tolerated, and driving off the track ends the episode with a penalty. Reaching the finish line adds a bonus. Exploration and learning rate automatically taper off as training progresses, so the policy settles instead of oscillating forever.

Click "Edit track shape" to reshape the curve yourself — it's a smooth bezier spline through six draggable points, so it stays a real function of left-to-right position (no loops or doubling back), which is what keeps the sensor logic well-defined.

Try this

1. Train a few hundred episodes, then click "Test learned policy" — this runs the robot with no exploration and no further learning, so you see exactly what it has learned.
2. Compare that to entering just 1 episode and training — with ε above 0, it still takes occasional random actions even mid-run, which is why single training runs can look shakier than test runs.
3. Change the off-track penalty, retrain, and test again — a harsher penalty should make it hug the center more cautiously through the curve.
4. Train on the default curve, then edit the track into a much sharper wave and test again without retraining — since the policy only reacts to color, not track shape, it often still works reasonably well on a new curve, unlike the maze policy, which broke completely when the goal moved.
